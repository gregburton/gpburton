## Initial Reaction

I first heard about TypeScript many years ago. People seemed to praise it and made
it seem as though it 'fixed' JavaScript. I took a quick peek at it and found a few
things that withheld my interest.

- I felt that there was still way too much to learn about <code className="js">JS</code>.
- I didn't understand the power of <code className="ts">TS</code> and the value of type checking.
- The syntax appeared chaotic and impossible to decipher.

So what if I could assign my types? Why was it worth bloating the code with this nonsense?

## The Double Take

I eventually started to notice certain changes as I continued to
use <code className="js">JS</code>. Documentation sites were starting to
provide <code className="ts">TS</code> snippets. Some of them even defaulted
to it. Sometimes certain questions I had were only available in <code className="ts">TS</code>.
I spent a good amount of time de-<code className="ts">TS</code>'ing various snippets. It was never
a fun time, but I learned more about the syntax during the process. I also
realized that VSCode started to autocomplete properties and methods as I typed.
This seemed like magic. How could it know what was attached to these things?
I learned that it was by the power of <code className="ts">TS</code>.

Maybe it was worth looking into again?

## The Tipping Point

Some time had passed, and I found myself in a conversation where I was
showing someone how easy it is to build a site with <code>create-react-app</code>
via the <a href="https://react.dev/" className="link">React Docs</a>. It's hard to describe how I felt
when I realized that you can't even find mention of the <code>create-react-app</code>
command! Instead, they recommend using someone else's framework. I did brief research
on the options and ended up using the first recommendation: <a href="https://nextjs.org/" className="link">Next.js</a>.
This was just before the release of their app router. I took a chance on it and only learned
minimal information on the \_pages router. Anyway, their documentation ONLY offered
snippets in <code className="ts">TS</code>. If Next.js truly was the future of web dev
and <code className="ts">TS</code> has that much of an impact on <code className="js">JS</code>,
then I really needed to get on board.

## Diving In

I really didn't enjoy using <code className="ts">TS</code> at first. There was quite
a bit to learn. I'm still only at the tip of the iceberg, but I'm definitely more
comfortable with it now.

I've recently re-written a few of my biggest projects into <code className="ts">TS</code>.
One of them remained client-only, and was also undergoing a transition from <code>create-react-app</code>
to <a href="https://vitejs.dev/" className="link">Vite</a>. Another one was an Express server
that was also undergoing the transition from CommonJS to ESM. A lot was happening at the time.

I learned a lot about <code className="ts">TS</code> in the process, and also grew to really enjoy it.
I also learned that my <code className="js">JS</code> code was filled with many errors as far
as <code className="ts">TS</code> was concerned. I feel like a madman for testing my luck
with <code className="js">JS</code> for so long.

It's nice to feel the power of <code className="ts">TS</code> and reap its rewards. It
may not be perfect, but it can only be an improvement from where I was.
