## The Beginning

I was gifted a Rubik's Cube by my girlfriend after mentioning a few times that
I've always wanted to solve one. These iconic puzzle toys are known by all and
are considered by most to be a mystery that can only be solved by magic.

In order to prevent an awesome gift from going unused and collecting dust and to
once and for all dive into this curious contraption, I found the
official <a href="https://rubiks.com/solve-guide" className="link">Rubik's Cube Solve Guide</a>.
I studied this guide for days while trying to make sense of the terminology
and algorithms.

I was able to make good progress, but would get stuck trying to make sense of
some of the steps and appropriate algorithms for the problem at hand. I decided
that I needed to _see_ how it was done. I searched up a few video tutorials, and
found one that made everything click. <a href="https://youtu.be/7Ron6MN45LY?si=IlJcezQlKpnyD42W" className="link">
This Beginner Tutorial by J Perm</a>
shed a much needed light on the confusion I was faced while attempting the cube.

## The Conclusion

Combining what I learned from the official guide with the visual step-by-step
allowed me to finally conquer the cube! It took a couple more days to really
commit the steps to memory, but I can now solve a 3x3 cube no matter how much
you may try to jumble it!

I realize that this can go much further. There are large competitions where
people compete to get the fastest times, people that solve them blindfolded,
and other sizes/formats of the puzzle. I am satisfied enough with knowing how
to solve it and don't plan on winning any competitions or getting really insane
with it.

I appreciate the cube for what it is. It is a very complicated puzzle if you
don't know how to solve it, but it becomes trivialized when you do. You can
take it as far as you'd like or decide to never learn how to solve it. I'm
glad I took the time to learn it. I keep it on my desk and give it a go every
now and then when I need to take a break from my current thoughts and/or give
my hands something to do. I'll even take it with me while running errands if
I expect to be held up.
