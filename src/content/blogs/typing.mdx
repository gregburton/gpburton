## Poking and Prodding

I'm proud to say that I am able to type without looking at the keyboard now. It only took
many years of desire to obtain the skill and frustration of not being able to do it.

It's embarrassing that it has taken me this long to learn this skill. I've been using computers
and their peripherals since I can remember. My typing skills first took off during my time
playing OldSchool RuneScape as a kid. You've gotta type fast if you want to stay relevant in
the conversation or show off your cool text effects. The methods I learned to type in this manner
stayed with me for many, many years.

I wouldn't necessarily say that I was a slow typer. I made use of most of my fingers and was
able to get out a decent amount of output in a relatively short amount of time. I was definitely
still faster than many adults, especially those who only used their pointer fingers.

## The Struggle

There have been so many times in life where it would've been very useful to be able to type
without looking at the keyboard. Many of these times occurred in college. I would try taking
notes from projected slides but sometimes I couldn't keep up due to having to keep redirecting
my attention and losing my place both on the board and on my own screen. It was at least double
the work and it could've been avoided if I just buckled down and learned how to type correctly!

Many people learn how to type during school. For me it was during middle school and I just couldn't
be bothered to learn such a skill at the time. They only tried making us learn for a class period
or two. We used these boxy keyboard covers that could be peeked under and well... I may or may not
have taken advantage of that flaw. Some people learn how to type even younger by being introduced to
various typing games. I steered clear of those in favor of action, rpg, or racing games instead.

It always fascinated me when I saw someone continue typing away at their keyboard while being engaged
in a conversation. Like the Rubik's Cube, it seemed like an impossible thing to do. I even started to
notice content creators on Twitch and YouTube type away without looking at their keyboards and decided
I needed to finally get down to business.

## Locking In

It was finally time to get the ball rolling. It all started with a Google search involving learning
how to type. A top result was a website called <a href="https://www.typing.com/">typing.com</a>.
I created an account and started learning. It taught how to place your hands and which fingers to use
for each letter. As with learning anything new, it was quite frustrating in the beginning. As soon as
I started to get the hang of it they would throw something new at me that really messed up my groove!
I ended up completing the website's Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced learning units. The website
says that my profile has an average speed of 31 WPM. Ahh, how far I've come since then.

The topic of touch typing randomly came up one time and my sister Michelle told me about a site she has
used to hone her typing skills called <a href="https://www.typelit.io/">typelit.io</a>. This site lets
you practice your typing skills by typing through works of literature. I decided to go in order and ended
up typing through _1984_, _Alice in Wonderland_, _Animal Farm_, and 77.8% of _Anna Karenina_
(it's a long book!). My WPM still varies here, but has increased with each book I've typed. I typed _1984_
with an average of 53 WPM and _Anna Karenina_ with a current average of 70 WPM. Typelit.io provides a nice
way to practice a skill while also reading books.

If I ever want to warm up or kill some time then I'll head over to <a href="https://monkeytype.com/">monkeytype</a>
and blast through an assortment of random words or quotes. There's something very satisfying about typing
on this platform. It tracks many stats, has a leveling system, and is quite customizable regarding themes and
typing preferences. It's not much to look at, but here's my <a href="https://monkeytype.com/profile/iCodeCSS">monkeytype profile</a>.

Another site that I used to practice typing is called <a href="https://monkeytype.com/">typeracer</a>.
This site allows you to engage in a competitive typing race against other people in the world. I would
play this on occasion, but mostly with my friend and girlfriend. She is really fast and can type over 100 WPM.
The only race I've won against her was when something prevented her from being able to type! Here's
my <a href="https://data.typeracer.com/pit/profile?user=icodehtml">typeracer profile</a> if you'd like to
take a peek.

## Now What?

So I've learned how to type without looking. I'm by no means the greatest at it and will continue to
keep practicing. I can always get faster, but more importantly, I can always get more accurate. My accuracy
is anywhere from 90-95%. It would be better if I tried typing slower, but I want to go fast! Now I can be
the one that types away while not looking and possibly impressing others.

It would be amazing to break the 100 WPM wall but I would have to really lock in and nonstop practice. I know
it is possible. Some people type 200-300 WPM and they all started where I started. As nice of a skill it is
to have, I don't need it to be better than it already is. It suits my needs and I'll improve upon it naturally
or forcibly if the need arises.
